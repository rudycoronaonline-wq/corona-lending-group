SECTION 2: WHAT IS A CONVENTIONAL LOAN
What Exactly Is a Conventional Loan?
A conventional loan is a mortgage that is not backed or insured by a government agency like the FHA, VA, or USDA. Instead, it follows guidelines set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, the two government-sponsored enterprises that purchase most conventional mortgages from lenders.
Because these loans meet standardized guidelines, lenders can sell them on the secondary market, which keeps money flowing and rates competitive. That is why conventional loans often offer better pricing than government-backed options for qualified borrowers.
There are two main categories:
Conforming Conventional Loans
These loans fall within the loan limits set by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), which change annually. In most California counties, the 2024 conforming loan limit is $766,550 for a single-family home. In high-cost areas like Los Angeles and Orange County, the limit can go up to $1,149,825.
Conforming loans offer the most competitive rates because lenders know they can easily sell them to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac.
Non-Conforming (Jumbo) Conventional Loans
When a loan exceeds the conforming limit, it becomes a jumbo loan. These are still conventional in structure, but they are held by the lender rather than sold, which means slightly stricter qualification requirements and modestly higher rates.
If you are purchasing a higher-value property in Southern California, we can explore jumbo options that still offer excellent terms.

SECTION 4: WHO IS IT FOR
Is a Conventional Loan Right for You?
A conventional loan tends to be the strongest option for buyers who meet the core qualifications. Here is an honest look at who benefits most:
You Are a Strong Candidate If...
Your credit score is 620 or higher (and the best rates kick in at 740+)
You have 3-20% available for a down payment
Your debt-to-income ratio is 45% or lower
You have a steady, verifiable income (W-2 or self-employed with clean returns)
You are purchasing a primary residence, second home, or investment property
You want to avoid paying FHA mortgage insurance for the life of the loan
You want a loan that offers flexibility as your financial situation evolves
A Conventional Loan May Not Be the Best Fit If...
Your credit score is below 620
You are a veteran eligible for a VA loan (VA loans are almost always a better deal)
You have minimal savings and want the lowest possible down payment
You are purchasing in a rural area and may qualify for USDA zero-down financing

SECTION 6: DOWN PAYMENT OPTIONS
Down Payment Options — From 3% to 20%
One of the biggest myths I hear is that you need 20% down to get a conventional loan. That has not been true for years. Here is the full picture:
3% Down — HomeReady and Home Possible Programs
Fannie Mae's HomeReady and Freddie Mac's Home Possible programs allow qualified buyers to put down as little as 3%. These programs are designed for moderate-income borrowers and first-time buyers, and they come with reduced mortgage insurance costs compared to standard conventional loans.
Minimum 620 credit score (660+ preferred)
Income limits apply based on county median income
Homebuyer education course required
Can use gift funds for the down payment
5% Down — Standard Conventional
The most common entry point for conventional loans. A 5% down payment gives you access to the full range of conventional products without the income restrictions of low-down-payment programs.
10% Down — Stronger Positioning
A 10% down payment reduces your loan-to-value ratio significantly, often resulting in lower PMI rates and stronger offers in competitive markets. Sellers in Southern California take buyers with more skin in the game more seriously.
20% Down — No PMI
The magic number. When you put 20% down, you eliminate Private Mortgage Insurance entirely, which typically saves $100-300 per month depending on your loan size. If you have the savings, this is where the long-term math often favors the larger down payment.
Down payment assistance programs exist even for conventional loans. Depending on your income and county, you may qualify for grants or second mortgages that help cover your down payment. Ask me what is available in your area.
SECTION 7: PRIVATE MORTGAGE INSURANCE (PMI)
Understanding Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)
If you put less than 20% down on a conventional loan, you will pay Private Mortgage Insurance. Let me give you a clear, honest breakdown of what that means for you.
What Is PMI?
PMI is a monthly insurance premium that protects the lender in case you default. It has nothing to do with protecting you as the borrower. But it is the price of entry for borrowers who are not yet at 20% equity.
How Much Does PMI Cost?
PMI typically costs between 0.5% and 1.5% of your loan amount per year, broken into monthly payments. Here is what that looks like in practice:

The Good News: PMI Is Not Forever
Unlike FHA loans, which require mortgage insurance for the life of the loan in most cases, conventional PMI automatically cancels when your loan balance reaches 80% of the original appraised value. You can also request cancellation proactively once you hit 80% LTV through appreciation or extra payments.
If Southern California home values continue to rise, your home may appreciate to 20% equity faster than you expect, triggering earlier PMI removal.
PMI Alternatives to Ask About
Lender-Paid PMI (LPMI): The lender pays your PMI in exchange for a slightly higher rate. No monthly PMI line item, but the cost is baked into your rate for the life of the loan.
Piggyback Loans (80/10/10): A first mortgage at 80%, a second mortgage at 10%, and 10% down. Eliminates PMI entirely with a creative structure.
Single Premium PMI: Pay all PMI upfront at closing instead of monthly. Works well if you have extra cash and plan to stay long-term.

SECTION 10: THE CONVENTIONAL LOAN PROCESS
How the Conventional Loan Process Works with Me
I know the mortgage process can feel overwhelming. Here is exactly what to expect when you work with me on a conventional loan, step by step:
Step 1: Free Consultation (Day 1)
We start with a conversation. You tell me where you are financially and what you are hoping to accomplish. I listen without judgment and without a sales pitch. By the end of that first call, you will know exactly what you qualify for and what your options are.
Step 2: Pre-Approval (Days 1-3)
Once you are ready to move forward, I collect your documentation and submit your file for formal pre-approval. For conventional loans, this typically includes:
Last 2 years of W-2s or tax returns
Most recent 2 pay stubs
2 months of bank statements
Government-issued ID and Social Security number
Information on current debts and assets
Most pre-approvals are ready within 1-3 business days. You will get a letter you can use immediately to make offers.
Step 3: Shop and Make an Offer (Your Timeline)
With your pre-approval in hand, you and your real estate agent can shop with confidence. When you find the right home and your offer is accepted, the formal loan process begins.
Step 4: Appraisal and Underwriting (Days 7-21)
Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home's value. Simultaneously, an underwriter reviews your full file. This is where any conditions are identified and resolved. I stay in constant communication with you throughout this phase so there are never any surprises.
Step 5: Clear to Close and Closing Day (Days 21-30)
Once underwriting approves your file, you receive a Clear to Close. You will get your final Closing Disclosure at least 3 business days before closing, showing your exact loan terms and costs. On closing day, you sign, funds transfer, and you get the keys.
My goal is always to get you to closing in 21-30 days. A prepared buyer with an organized file makes that happen. I will tell you exactly what I need and when, so the process moves smoothly.

SECTION 14: CONVENTIONAL LOAN FAQ
Conventional Loan Frequently Asked Questions
What credit score do I need for a conventional loan?
The minimum is 620, but I always recommend aiming for 680 or higher to access a broader range of products and better rates. At 740 and above, you will typically see the most competitive pricing. If your score is below 620 right now, let's talk. There are steps you can take to get there faster than you might think.
Can I use gift money for my conventional loan down payment?
Yes, with conditions. For a primary residence with 20% down, the entire down payment can be a gift. With less than 20% down, you may need to contribute a small portion from your own funds. The gift must come from an approved donor (immediate family members are standard), and a signed gift letter is required. I will walk you through the specifics for your situation.
How much can I borrow with a conventional loan?
It depends on your county. In Los Angeles and Orange County, the 2024 conforming limit is $1,149,825 for a single-family home. In Riverside and San Bernardino counties, the limit is $766,550. If you need to borrow above the conforming limit, we look at jumbo conventional options.
Can I get a conventional loan if I am self-employed?
Absolutely. Self-employed borrowers qualify using their last two years of personal and business tax returns. The key is that lenders use your net income after deductions, which can sometimes show lower than your actual cash flow. If that is your situation, I may recommend a bank statement loan instead, which uses your real deposits rather than your taxable income. We will run the numbers both ways.
How long does it take to get a conventional loan?
Most conventional purchases close in 21-30 days from accepted offer. Refinances can sometimes take a few days longer. The biggest factor in speed is how quickly we have your documentation. When clients come in organized and prepared, we close fast.
What happens if the home does not appraise at the purchase price?
This is more common in competitive markets. If the appraisal comes in below the purchase price, you have a few options: negotiate a price reduction with the seller, bring additional cash to cover the gap, or in some cases, challenge the appraisal with a Reconsideration of Value. I will guide you through whatever the situation calls for.
Can I pay off my conventional loan early?
Yes. Conventional loans do not have prepayment penalties. You can make extra payments, pay it off early, or refinance at any time without any penalty from the loan structure itself.
When does my PMI go away?
PMI on a conventional loan is required to be automatically canceled when your loan balance reaches 78% of the original appraised value. You can request cancellation at 80% LTV. You can also reach 80% equity faster through home appreciation, and in that case, a new appraisal may allow you to remove PMI ahead of schedule. I will help you track this.
