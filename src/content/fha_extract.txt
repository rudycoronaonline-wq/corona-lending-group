SECTION 3: WHAT IS AN FHA LOAN
What Is an FHA Loan?
An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration, a division of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). FHA does not lend money directly. Instead, it provides insurance to approved lenders, protecting them against losses if a borrower defaults.
Because lenders carry less risk, they can offer FHA loans with more flexible qualification standards than conventional mortgages. That flexibility is passed on directly to you as the borrower.
FHA loans have been around since 1934 and have helped over 50 million Americans purchase or refinance homes. They are not a niche product or a last resort. They are a mainstream, well-established path to homeownership used by millions of buyers every year.
FHA loans are not a sign that you are behind. They are a sign that you are smart enough to use the right tool for your situation. There is no shame in taking the path that works. The goal is getting into your home.

How to Reduce What You Pay at Closing
FHA has some of the most generous rules around seller contributions and closing cost help:
Seller concessions: The seller can contribute up to 6% of the purchase price toward your closing costs. This is significantly higher than conventional loans allow at most down payment levels. In negotiations, asking for seller concessions is a real strategy that can dramatically reduce the cash you bring to closing.
Gift funds: All of your down payment and all of your closing costs can come from gift funds from an eligible donor (family members, employer, charitable organization). A gift letter is required.
Down payment assistance programs: FHA is compatible with CalHFA and many county-level assistance programs that provide grants or forgivable second mortgages for both down payment and closing costs. Ask me specifically what programs are available in your target county.
Lender credits: In some cases, a slightly higher interest rate can be exchanged for a lender credit that covers part of your closing costs. This makes sense for buyers with limited cash but who can handle a marginally higher monthly payment.
SECTION 9: DOWN PAYMENT ASSISTANCE AND CalHFA
Down Payment Assistance: You May Not Need as Much as You Think
One of the most underused opportunities in California is down payment assistance programs, and FHA loans are specifically designed to work with them.
CalHFA MyHome Assistance Program
The California Housing Finance Agency offers the MyHome Assistance Program, which provides a deferred-payment junior loan to cover down payment and closing costs on CalHFA first mortgages. It works like this:
Amount: Up to 3.5% of the purchase price (enough to cover your entire FHA down payment)
Payment: Deferred until you sell, refinance, or pay off the first mortgage
Income limits: Apply based on county; contact me for current limits in your area
Purchase price limits: Apply; contact me for current limits
First-time buyer requirement: Typically required (defined as not owning a home in the past 3 years)
Homebuyer education: Required online or in-person course before closing
CalHFA Dream For All Shared Appreciation Loan
For eligible first-generation homebuyers, this program provides up to 20% of the purchase price as a down payment loan. In exchange, CalHFA shares in a portion of the future appreciation when the home is eventually sold.
This program is popular and often oversubscribed, so availability varies by funding cycle. Ask me about current status and eligibility when we talk.

Many Southern California counties and cities have their own assistance programs that work alongside FHA:

SECTION 11: FHA STREAMLINE REFINANCE
Already Have an FHA Loan? The Streamline Refinance
If you currently have an FHA loan and interest rates have dropped since you closed, the FHA Streamline Refinance is one of the simplest refinance options available anywhere in the mortgage market.
What Makes It "Streamlined"?
No new appraisal required in most cases
Reduced income documentation (no pay stubs or tax returns required in many cases)
No minimum credit score requirement from FHA (lenders may set their own)
Faster closing timeline than a full refinance
Lower upfront MIP rate (0.01% UFMIP vs. 1.75% on a new FHA loan)
Requirements
Must have an existing FHA loan in good standing
Must be current on payments with no 30-day lates in the past 6 months and no more than one in the past 12 months
Loan must be at least 210 days old from the first payment due date
Refinance must result in a net tangible benefit (lower rate, lower payment, or move from adjustable to fixed)
If you bought your home in the last few years when rates were higher and rates have since come down, the FHA Streamline Refinance could lower your monthly payment significantly with very little paperwork. Contact me and I will pull your current loan details and run the numbers.

SECTION 13: FHA MULTI-UNIT STRATEGY
The FHA House Hack: Buy a 2-4 Unit Property and Let Tenants Help Pay Your Mortgage
This is one of my favorite strategies to share with buyers who want to build wealth from the very first property they purchase, and FHA makes it incredibly accessible.
FHA allows you to purchase a 2, 3, or 4-unit property with as little as 3.5% down, as long as you live in one of the units as your primary residence. The rental income from the other units can be counted toward your qualifying income, which often allows you to qualify for a much larger loan than you could on your own.
How It Works
You purchase a duplex, triplex, or fourplex with 3.5% down using an FHA loan.
You move into one unit and rent out the others.
Rental income from the other units can offset or even cover your entire mortgage payment.
You build equity while tenants help pay down your loan.
In a few years, you may be able to move out, keep the property as a full investment, and purchase your next primary residence.
Example: A buyer purchases a triplex in Riverside for $620,000 with 3.5% down ($21,700). The two rental units bring in $1,800 each per month ($3,600 total). The total mortgage payment is $4,200. After rental income, the buyer's effective housing cost is $600 per month. This buyer is essentially living nearly for free while building equity in a property worth over $600,000. That changes lives.
FHA loan limits for 2-4 unit properties are significantly higher than for single-family homes, as shown in the loan limits table above. This strategy is available at price points many buyers do not expect to be able to reach with FHA.

Step 4: Finding Your Home (Your Timeline)
With your pre-approval in hand, you and your real estate agent shop for your home. Once your offer is accepted, the formal loan process begins. I will coordinate directly with your agent throughout this phase.
Step 5: FHA Appraisal (Days 7 to 20 after offer acceptance)
Your lender orders an FHA appraisal from a HUD-approved appraiser. This appraisal serves two purposes: it determines the home's market value AND confirms the property meets FHA Minimum Property Standards (MPS).
If the appraiser identifies required repairs, we will address them. Options include:
Seller completes repairs before closing
Price reduction to account for repairs you complete after closing
Repair escrow holdback in some cases
FHA 203(k) renovation loan if the home needs significant work
Step 6: Underwriting (Days 20 to 35)
An underwriter reviews your complete file and the appraisal report. This is where any remaining conditions are identified and resolved. I stay in constant contact with you throughout this phase and handle everything I can on your behalf.
Step 7: Clear to Close and Closing Day
Once underwriting approves the file, you receive your Clear to Close notification. You will get a final Closing Disclosure at least 3 business days before closing, showing all loan terms and costs. On closing day, you sign, funds transfer, and you get the keys to your new home.
From accepted offer to closing, FHA loans typically take 30 to 45 days. With a prepared buyer and an organized file, I target the shorter end of that range.

SECTION 18: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
FHA Loan Frequently Asked Questions
Can I get an FHA loan with bad credit?
FHA allows credit scores as low as 580 for the 3.5% down payment option, and as low as 500 with a 10% down payment. That said, not all lenders offer the 500-579 tier. More importantly, a low credit score does not always mean you cannot qualify. I look at your full credit picture, not just the number. If your recent payment history is strong, even a score in the 580s can work well with FHA. If your score is below 580 right now, I will help you map a realistic path to get there.
Can I use an FHA loan if I have had a bankruptcy or foreclosure?
Yes, with waiting periods. For a Chapter 7 bankruptcy, FHA requires a 2-year waiting period from the discharge date. For a Chapter 13, you may be eligible after 1 year of on-time payments with court approval. For a foreclosure, the standard waiting period is 3 years. In some cases of extenuating circumstances, these periods can be shortened. I will walk through your specific history and tell you exactly where you stand.
Can I use an FHA loan to buy a condo?
Yes, but only if the condominium project is on the FHA-approved condo list maintained by HUD. Not all condo complexes are approved. If you have a specific condo in mind, I will check its FHA approval status immediately. If it is not currently approved, there is also an FHA spot approval process available for certain projects. I will let you know what options exist.
I am not a first-time buyer. Can I still use an FHA loan?
Yes. FHA is not limited to first-time buyers. The only requirement related to homeownership is that FHA must be used for a primary residence. You cannot own another FHA-financed home at the same time, but if you have owned a home before and are buying a new primary residence, you can absolutely use FHA again.
How long does it take to close an FHA loan?
From accepted offer to closing, most FHA loans close in 30 to 45 days. The FHA appraisal process, which includes the property condition review, is often what adds time compared to conventional. Having all your documents ready from day one and choosing a property in good condition are the two biggest factors in a faster close.
Does FHA require a home inspection?
FHA requires an FHA appraisal, which includes a review of the property's condition, but it does not require a separate home inspection. However, I always recommend getting an independent home inspection regardless of loan type. The FHA appraisal protects the lender. A home inspection protects you. They are different things, and both matter.
Can the seller pay my closing costs on an FHA loan?
Yes. FHA allows sellers to contribute up to 6% of the purchase price toward your closing costs, prepaid items, and discount points. This is one of the most buyer-friendly features of the program. In your purchase offer, we can negotiate seller concessions that significantly reduce the cash you need at closing.
What happens if the FHA appraisal comes in low?
If the appraised value comes in below the purchase price, you have a few options: ask the seller to reduce the price to the appraised value, pay the difference between the appraised value and the purchase price in cash, or walk away if the purchase contract has an appraisal contingency. I will help you navigate whatever situation arises.
Will I be stuck with FHA mortgage insurance forever?
Not necessarily. If you put less than 10% down, FHA MIP stays for the life of the loan unless you refinance. But once you have built 20% or more equity in your home, through a combination of payments and appreciation, you can refinance into a conventional loan and eliminate the mortgage insurance entirely. Many buyers use FHA to get into their home now with a plan to refinance to conventional in 3 to 5 years. I will help you think through that strategy during our consultation.
Can I have a co-borrower on an FHA loan who does not live in the home?
Yes. FHA allows non-occupant co-borrowers. This is a powerful option for buyers whose income alone is not sufficient to qualify. A parent, sibling, or other family member can co-sign the loan without living in the property. Their income counts toward qualification. Their credit and debt also factor in, so we will evaluate the full picture of both borrowers together.
