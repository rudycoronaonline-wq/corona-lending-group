SECTION 3: WHAT IS A HARD MONEY LOAN

What Is a Hard Money Loan?

A hard money loan is a short-term, asset-based loan secured by real property. Unlike conventional mortgages, which underwrite the borrower's income, employment, and credit history in detail, hard money lenders primarily evaluate the value of the property being used as collateral and the exit strategy for repaying the loan.

The name comes from the collateral itself: hard assets. The loan is backed by hard real estate, not soft promises about future income.

Hard money loans are typically originated by private lenders, private equity funds, and specialty finance companies, not by traditional banks or credit unions. Because these lenders operate outside the conventional regulatory framework, they can move significantly faster and with far greater flexibility.

SECTION 4: WHEN HARD MONEY IS THE RIGHT TOOL

When Does a Hard Money Loan Make Sense?

Hard money is not the right tool for every deal. But for certain situations, it is the only tool that works. Here is an honest breakdown of when hard money shines and when it does not:

Fix-and-Flip Acquisitions

This is the most common use case. An investor finds a distressed property, often underpriced because of its condition, and needs to purchase it fast and fund the renovation. Banks will not finance properties with significant deferred maintenance or safety issues. Hard money lenders will, because they are lending against the After-Repair Value (ARV), which reflects what the property is worth after the renovation is complete.

Example: A property is purchased for $320,000 in distressed condition. Renovation budget is $80,000. Estimated ARV after repairs is $550,000. A hard money lender offers a loan of up to 70% of ARV, or $385,000, which covers the purchase price and contributes toward renovation costs. The investor brings the gap and the profit potential is built into the deal from day one.

Bridge Financing

Bridge loans fill a gap between where you are now and where you need to be with permanent financing. Common bridge scenarios include:

Purchasing a new property before your existing property sells, when you cannot qualify for both mortgages at once

Acquiring a property that does not yet meet the occupancy or condition requirements for conventional or DSCR financing, then stabilizing it and refinancing

Closing quickly on an opportunity while permanent financing is being arranged through a longer process

Buying out a partner or estate and needing short-term cash before refinancing the property long-term

Distressed Properties That Do Not Qualify Conventionally

Conventional lenders and even many DSCR lenders require properties to be habitable and in good condition at the time of closing. Homes with significant deferred maintenance, fire damage, flood damage, structural issues, or code violations will not pass conventional appraisals. Hard money lenders focus on value, not condition, which opens up an entire category of below-market opportunities.

Time-Sensitive Acquisitions

Real estate auctions, probate sales, estate liquidations, and off-market deals from motivated sellers often require fast closings. A seller who needs to close in 10 days cannot wait for a conventional 45-day process. Hard money can close in that window, which makes your offer competitive in situations where cash buyers typically dominate.

Non-Bankable Borrowers with Strong Deals

An investor with excellent real estate judgment but imperfect personal credit, limited employment history, or complex tax returns may struggle to qualify for conventional financing despite having a genuinely profitable deal in hand. Hard money evaluates the deal first and the borrower second. If the property supports the loan, many lenders will work with borrowers that conventional lenders would turn away.

Construction and Ground-Up Development

Some hard money lenders offer construction loans for ground-up projects or major gut renovations. Funds are disbursed in draws as construction milestones are met, similar to conventional construction lending but with faster approval and more flexibility around the borrower's financial profile.

SECTION 5: HOW HARD MONEY LOANS ARE UNDERWRITTEN

How Hard Money Lenders Evaluate a Deal

Understanding how a hard money lender thinks about your deal is essential to presenting it well and getting the best terms. Here are the factors that matter most:

1. After-Repair Value (ARV)

For fix-and-flip and renovation loans, ARV is the single most important number in the deal. It is what the property will be worth after all planned improvements are complete, based on comparable sales in the area.

Hard money lenders typically lend 65% to 75% of ARV. Everything else in the deal flows from this number.

3. The Exit Strategy

This is non-negotiable for every hard money lender. Because the loan is short-term by design, the lender wants to know exactly how and when you plan to repay it. The two primary exit strategies are:

Sell the property (flip): After renovation is complete, the property is listed and sold. Loan is repaid at closing from sale proceeds.

Refinance to permanent financing: After renovation and stabilization, the property is refinanced into a DSCR loan, conventional investment loan, or other long-term financing.

A weak or vague exit strategy will either kill the deal with a lender or result in worse terms. I help my clients think through and articulate a clear, credible exit before we approach any lender.

4. Borrower Experience and Track Record

Hard money lenders do not require income verification, but they do consider your experience as a real estate investor. A borrower who has completed multiple successful fix-and-flip projects is viewed as significantly lower risk than a first-time investor, regardless of credit score.

First-time investors can still access hard money financing, but they may face lower LTV ratios, higher rates, or additional requirements. If this is your first deal, being honest about your situation upfront allows me to match you to the right lender.

5. Credit Score and Financial Background

Credit score matters less in hard money than in conventional lending, but it is not ignored. Most hard money lenders want to see a minimum score in the 550 to 620 range. A higher score, 660 or above, typically results in better rates and terms even in the hard money space. Significant derogatory history, including active bankruptcies, multiple recent foreclosures, or outstanding judgments, will affect lender appetite.

6. Scope of Work and Renovation Budget

For renovation loans, lenders want to see a detailed scope of work with itemized costs. This demonstrates that you understand what the property needs, have realistic cost estimates, and have a credible plan for execution. Lenders often have their own inspectors verify the scope before funding and throughout the draw process.

Draw Schedules for Renovation Loans

When a hard money loan includes renovation funding, the construction portion is typically not advanced all at once. It is held in reserve and disbursed in draws as work is completed. Here is how draws typically work:

Initial draw: A portion of renovation funds released at closing or upon first inspection (often 10% to 25%)

Progress draws: As renovation milestones are completed, the lender sends an inspector to verify work before releasing the next tranche of funds

Final draw: Last portion released upon substantial completion and final inspection

Draw timing and inspection requirements vary by lender. Some lenders have their own inspectors. Some use third-party inspection services. The typical draw inspection costs $150 to $300 and is paid by the borrower. I will explain the specific draw process for any lender before you commit to them.

SECTION 10: HARD MONEY IN THE SOUTHERN CALIFORNIA MARKET

Hard Money Lending in the Southern California Market

Southern California is one of the most active fix-and-flip and investment property markets in the country. High property values, consistent demand, and strong appreciation have made it a destination for real estate investors from across the state and the nation.

Here is what makes SoCal unique from a hard money financing perspective:

Higher Loan Amounts

Because Southern California property values are among the highest in the nation, hard money loan amounts are correspondingly larger. A fix-and-flip in Los Angeles or Orange County might require a loan of $600,000 to $1.2 million, compared to $200,000 to $400,000 in lower-cost markets. Not all hard money lenders are set up to fund at these levels. I work with lenders who specifically operate in the high-value SoCal market and are comfortable with these loan sizes.

Competitive for Speed

Distressed properties in Southern California attract cash buyers and experienced flippers in large numbers. Getting a hard money loan that can close in 10 to 14 days can be the difference between getting the deal and watching someone else take it. Speed is the core value proposition of hard money in this market, and I work with lenders who understand urgency.

ARV Reliability

ARV underwriting requires reliable comparable sales data. In active Southern California markets, this data is generally robust and appraisers have strong comparable transactions to reference. This means lenders are more comfortable advancing at 70% to 75% of ARV in our market than they might be in slower or more rural areas with limited comps.

Exit Options

The Southern California market supports multiple exit strategies. For flippers, there is consistent buyer demand across most submarkets. For investors who want to hold, DSCR refinancing is accessible because rental demand supports strong cash flow ratios. This exit flexibility gives lenders more confidence in SoCal deals and can result in more favorable terms.

SECTION 11: HARD MONEY AND THE BRRRR STRATEGY

Hard Money and the BRRRR Strategy

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. It is one of the most powerful wealth-building strategies in real estate investing, and hard money is the financing engine that makes it work.

How BRRRR Works with Hard Money

Buy: Use a hard money loan to acquire a distressed property below market value. The speed and flexibility of hard money lets you compete with cash buyers.

Rehab: Use the renovation draws from the hard money loan to fund the renovation. Complete the work to bring the property to rentable condition.

Rent: Lease the property to a qualified tenant. Establish rental income and a rent roll.

Refinance: Once the property is stabilized with a tenant, refinance out of the hard money loan into a DSCR loan or conventional investment loan. The refinance is based on the new appraised value after renovation, which should be significantly higher than your purchase price and total costs.

Repeat: Use the equity pulled from the refinance as the down payment on the next property. Rinse and repeat.

Example: Purchase distressed property with hard money for $280,000. Renovate for $70,000 (total cost: $350,000). Property appraises at $500,000 after renovation. Refinance into a DSCR loan at 75% LTV = $375,000. You pull out $375,000, pay off the $350,000 in total costs, and have $25,000 remaining plus a cash-flowing rental property that appreciates over time. That $25,000 is your recycled capital for the next deal.

The BRRRR strategy works best when you buy right, renovate efficiently, and choose markets with strong rental demand. Southern California checks every box. I help investors structure the hard money financing for the acquisition and renovation, then transition them into DSCR or conventional permanent financing when the property is ready. I stay with you through the full cycle.

SECTION 12: WHAT TO WATCH OUT FOR IN HARD MONEY

The Risks of Hard Money Lending: What You Need to Know

I believe in telling the full truth, even when it is uncomfortable. Hard money loans carry risks that conventional loans do not, and every investor deserves to understand them clearly before committing to one.

Higher Cost of Capital

The rates and fees on hard money loans are significantly higher than conventional financing. If a renovation takes longer than expected or the sale takes longer than anticipated, the carrying costs can erode your profit quickly. Always build a buffer into your timeline projections.

Short Loan Terms

Hard money loans mature quickly, often in 6 to 12 months. If your project is not complete or your exit strategy is not in place when the loan matures, you may face an extension fee, and some lenders will begin foreclosure proceedings if the loan is not resolved. Always know your exact maturity date and have your exit strategy in motion well before it arrives.

Variable Lender Quality

The hard money space is less regulated than conventional mortgage lending. Not all hard money lenders are reputable, transparent, or experienced in Southern California. Some use predatory terms, hidden fees, or slow draw processes that derail your renovation timeline. Choosing the right lender is as important as finding the right deal.

This is one of the most important reasons to work with a knowledgeable broker rather than approaching hard money lenders cold. I have vetted relationships with reputable hard money lenders in Southern California and know which ones close on time, fund draws efficiently, and deal honestly with their borrowers. I protect my clients from the lenders they should avoid.

Renovation Cost Overruns

In real estate renovation, unexpected costs are not unusual. They are almost inevitable. Mold behind drywall. Plumbing that needs replacement. Structural issues not visible on initial walkthrough. If your renovation budget runs over and you exhaust your hard money draw funds, you need to bring cash to cover the gap. Always include a 10% to 15% contingency in your renovation budget from the beginning.

Market Timing Risk

A property that appraises at $550,000 ARV today may sell for less if the market softens during a 9-month renovation and marketing period. Hard money is almost always a short-term hold, which limits your market timing risk compared to long-term holds. But it is not zero. Know your local market and have a realistic understanding of current buyer demand in your target price range.

SECTION 13: THE HARD MONEY LOAN PROCESS

How the Hard Money Loan Process Works with Me

Hard money moves fast. Here is exactly what to expect from the moment you bring me a deal to the day you close:

Step 1: Deal Review Call (Same Day)

You bring me the deal. I review the numbers: purchase price, ARV estimate, renovation scope, and your exit strategy. I tell you quickly whether the deal has merit for hard money financing and what loan amount and terms to expect. This is a fast, focused conversation designed to save you time.

Step 2: Lender Matching (Days 1 to 2)

Not every hard money lender is right for every deal. I match your deal to the lender whose program fits best based on property type, loan size, LTV, borrower experience, and required closing speed. I submit a brief deal summary to the right lender and get preliminary terms back quickly.

Step 3: Term Sheet and Commitment (Days 2 to 5)

The lender issues a term sheet or letter of intent outlining the proposed loan amount, rate, points, term, and conditions. I review this with you line by line. If anything needs negotiating or clarifying, that happens before you accept.

Step 4: Application and Documentation (Days 3 to 7)

Hard money documentation is minimal compared to conventional financing. Here is what most lenders require:

Government-issued photo ID for all borrowers

Purchase contract (for acquisitions) or title information (for refinances)

Detailed scope of work with itemized renovation costs (for rehab loans)

Contractor information and bids (some lenders require licensed contractor)

Entity documents if borrowing in LLC: Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement, EIN letter

Brief track record or portfolio history (list of previous rehab projects or investment properties owned)

Proof of down payment funds: 2 months bank statements or asset statement

No tax returns, no W-2s, no employment verification

Step 5: Appraisal or BPO (Days 5 to 10)

The lender orders an appraisal or Broker Price Opinion (BPO) to confirm the as-is value and/or ARV. Some hard money lenders use their own in-house team for this step. Appraisals take 5 to 10 days. BPOs can be faster. This is often the primary determinant of the closing timeline.

Step 6: Closing (Days 10 to 21)

Once the appraisal is in and the lender has reviewed all documents, closing is scheduled. Most hard money closings take place at a title or escrow company. Funds are wired and you take ownership of the property.

A fix-and-flip investor purchasing a distressed property that needs renovation

An investor who needs to close in 7 to 21 days to secure a deal

Hard Money Is Not the Right Fit If You Are...

Purchasing a primary residence you plan to live in (hard money is for investment only in most cases)

Buying a move-in ready property that qualifies for conventional or DSCR financing (pay the lower rate)

Uncertain about your renovation costs or exit strategy (hard money on a bad deal makes things worse)

Unable to comfortably afford the higher monthly interest payments during the renovation period

Not prepared for the possibility of extension fees if your project takes longer than planned

The most important thing I can tell you about hard money: it is a tool, not a strategy. The strategy is your deal. The exit plan. The renovation scope. The buyer or tenant waiting on the other end. SECTION 17: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Hard Money Loan Frequently Asked Questions

What credit score do I need for a hard money loan?

Most hard money lenders in Southern California look for a minimum credit score in the 550 to 620 range. However, the emphasis is far more on the deal itself than on credit. A strong deal with a low LTV can sometimes get approved with a score below 600 at certain lenders. Conversely, a marginal deal does not get saved by a 750 credit score. If you are concerned about your credit, bring me the deal numbers first and let us see where we stand.

Can I get a hard money loan for my first flip?

Yes. Many hard money lenders will work with first-time flippers, though they may offer slightly lower LTV ratios or require a more detailed scope of work to compensate for the lack of track record. Being well-prepared helps significantly. Having a detailed renovation scope, a licensed contractor ready, and a clear exit strategy all signal to the lender that you have done your homework. I have helped first-time investors get their first hard money deal across the finish line.

How fast can a hard money loan actually close?

In Southern California, a well-prepared hard money deal with a cooperative title company can close in as few as 7 to 10 days. A typical deal closes in 14 to 21 days. The primary variable is the appraisal or BPO timeline. Some lenders use in-house valuation which speeds the process. If you have a deal that needs to close in 10 days, tell me upfront and I will prioritize lenders with the fastest appraisal and underwriting processes.

Do hard money lenders lend on the purchase price or the ARV?

Both numbers matter. Most lenders will not lend more than 90% of the purchase price regardless of ARV. And they will not lend more than 65% to 75% of ARV regardless of the purchase price. The loan amount is calculated based on whichever limit is lower. The ARV is the most important number for determining maximum loan size, but the purchase price sets a floor on how much the lender will advance against that ARV.

Can I borrow in my LLC with a hard money loan?

Yes. In fact, most experienced investors prefer to close in an LLC for liability protection and tax structuring. The vast majority of hard money lenders accept LLC borrowers. You will need to provide your Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement, and EIN letter. Some lenders may require a personal guarantee from the managing member even on LLC loans. I will clarify the guarantee requirements for any lender before you commit.

What happens if my project goes over budget?

If your renovation costs exceed your initial scope and you exhaust the renovation draws in your hard money loan, you will need to bring cash to cover the gap. This is why a 10% to 15% contingency buffer in your renovation budget is not optional, it is essential. If you need additional financing mid-project, some lenders will consider a modification, but this is not guaranteed. The cleaner your initial scope and budget, the less likely you are to face this situation.

What happens if I cannot sell or refinance before my loan matures?

Most hard money lenders will offer an extension if you are making progress and the project is on track. Extensions typically cost 0.5% to 2% of the loan amount and usually extend the term by 3 to 6 months. It is important to request an extension before the loan matures, not after. If the loan goes into default, the lender has the right to begin foreclosure proceedings. Understanding and managing your timeline is one of the most critical responsibilities of the borrower.

Can I use a hard money loan to buy land?

Some hard money lenders offer land loans, but they are less common and come with lower LTV ratios, typically 50% to 65% of land value. The lender needs a credible development plan and clear path to an exit. Raw land with no development plan is very difficult to finance through hard money. Entitled land with permits in process is much more financeable. If you are looking at a land acquisition, tell me about the full picture and I will assess whether a hard money lender would engage on it.

How do renovation draws work?

Renovation funds are held in reserve by the lender and released in draws as work is completed. When you are ready to draw funds, you submit a draw request and the lender sends an inspector to verify that the claimed work has been completed. Once the inspection confirms completion, funds are wired to you, typically within 24 to 72 hours. Most rehab projects have 3 to 6 draw inspections. Each inspection costs $150 to $300 and is paid by the borrower. I will walk you through the specific draw process for whichever lender we use.
