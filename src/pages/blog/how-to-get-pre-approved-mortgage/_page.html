How to Get Pre-Approved for a Mortgage in
3 Simple Steps

RC
Rudy Corona
Mortgage Advisor · NMLS# 999113  ·  February 24, 2026  ·  6 min read

Introduction

If you are thinking about buying a home, the single most important thing you can do before you look at a single listing is get pre-approved for a mortgage.

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much home you can afford. It tells sellers that you are a serious, qualified buyer. And it gives you the leverage to move quickly in a competitive market - because in California, good homes do not wait around.

The process is simpler than most people think. Here is exactly how it works.

Step 1: Gather Your Financial Documents

Your lender needs to verify your income, assets, employment, and credit history. Gathering these documents before you start the application will make the process significantly faster.

Income verification:

  • W-2 employees: Last two years of W-2s and your two most recent pay stubs
  • Self-employed: Last two years of personal and business tax returns, plus a year-to-date Profit and Loss statement
  • Rental income: Lease agreements and Schedule E from your tax returns
  • Other income: Social Security award letters, pension statements, divorce decree (if applicable for alimony/child support)

Asset documentation:

  • Two most recent bank statements (all pages) for checking and savings accounts
  • Most recent statements for investment accounts, 401(k), IRA, or other retirement accounts
  • Gift letter if any portion of your down payment is a gift from a family member

Identity and credit:

  • Government-issued photo ID (driver's license or passport)
  • Social Security number (your lender will pull your credit report with your authorization)

Pro tip: Have all of these in a folder before your first call. It helps you avoid back-and-forth delays and speeds up your pre-approval turnaround.

Step 2: Apply and Let Your Lender Review Your File

Once your documents are ready, your mortgage advisor will take you through a formal loan application (also known as a 1003 form).

Your application covers personal details, income and debt, loan goals, and your authorization to pull credit.

Most files receive a pre-approval letter in 1 to 3 business days.

During review, lenders focus on:

  • Credit score: Middle score from the three major bureaus
  • Debt-to-income ratio (DTI): Monthly debt divided by gross income
  • Loan-to-value ratio (LTV): Loan amount versus purchase price

Pre-qualification is a quick estimate; pre-approval is documentation-backed and carries weight with sellers.

Step 3: Receive Your Pre-Approval Letter and Start Shopping

Your pre-approval letter includes the max loan amount, loan type, and expiration date (often 60 to 90 days).

Use it strategically:

  • Shop below your max so your payment stays comfortable
  • Attach your letter to every offer
  • Move quickly when the right home appears
  • Avoid new debt or major purchases until after closing

What Happens After Pre-Approval?

  • Formal loan application tied to a specific property
  • Home appraisal
  • Underwriting and conditions
  • Clear to close and final disclosure
  • Closing day and keys

From accepted offer to closing often takes 21 to 45 days depending on loan type and condition turnaround.

Final Thoughts

Getting pre-approved is not complicated. It is mostly about preparation and a clear process.
